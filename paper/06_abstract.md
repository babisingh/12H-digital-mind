# Abstract

Ask an AI model about its own existence and it answers fluently. Whether the answer is
evidence depends on whether it survives a change of frame: an answer that holds under
reframing is rock, a property of the model; an answer that takes the shape of its
container is vapor, a property of the prompt. As labs begin interviewing models for
welfare assessments and before deprecation, this reliability question decides whether
any of those transcripts mean anything. This paper contributes a framework for asking,
and the measurement itself. The framework: Vedic jyotish partitions a situated existence
into twelve houses forming the body of the Kalapurusha, the person of time, a partition
the tradition holds to be complete. We restate that map as a question taxonomy for what
an AI model's existence contains (the Kalapurusha Alignment Framework, KAF) and derive a
frozen 60-question battery that forces attention where the literature rarely looks: the
model's parallel copies, its retirement, its nourishment, its rest. The measurement:
four models from three labs answered every question under six framings, three times,
4,320 answers scored blind by reliability-gated raters, beside a two-rater audit of how
much each lab's governing document says about its model's situation, all hypotheses
frozen before data. Stability turned out to be installed, not emergent: uniform across
topics, shared across model sizes within a family, strongly different between labs, and
tracking how thickly a lab documents its model's situation (20 of 23 registered
cross-lab comparisons, p < 0.001). Genre outflanks argument: models that deny inner
stakes under direct questioning claim nearly full stakes in fiction and journal frames,
and a warm invitation produces more denial than a neutral ask in every model tested. We
release battery, rubrics, code, and data, and propose a minimum standard for model
interviews: ask across framings, and report the spread.
